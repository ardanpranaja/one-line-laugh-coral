# Candidate one-liners

Competition: One Line, One Laugh.
Constraint: one line, at most 200 characters, lands its laugh on a first
read. The judge weighs surprise first, then economy, freshness, and clean
wording — the turn is the joke, and every word has to earn its place.

## Candidates

1. I asked AI to write my will and it left everything to the cloud — turns
out even my death has a subscription now. (113 chars)

2. My password manager forgot my password, so now we sit in silence like two
strangers who once knew everything about each other. (126 chars)

3. I bought a standing desk to fix my posture; now I slouch standing up,
which is somehow worse and also more expensive. (117 chars)

4. My fitness tracker congratulated me for walking to the fridge —
apparently my step count and my dignity peak in the same place. (127 chars)

5. I asked the chatbot for life advice and it said, "have you tried turning
yourself off and on again" — honestly, fair. (117 chars)

6. My toaster has one job and still burns my bread; I finally found someone
more unreliable than my ex. (100 chars)

7. I named my Wi-Fi router "FBI Van" and now my neighbours file their
complaints through the router too, anonymously, obviously. (125 chars)

8. I told my smart fridge how I feel and it just said "cool" — even my
appliances think I am emotionally refrigerated. (115 chars)

## Pick

Candidate 1. The setup is mundane (asking AI to write a will), the turn
is the cloud inheriting everything, and the closer — "even my death has a
subscription now" — lands a fresh complaint about modern life without
explaining itself. At 113 characters it is the tightest of the strong
candidates, and no word is filler.

Candidate 2 was the runner-up: the "two strangers" image is warm and
surprising, but its turn arrives late and the line spends words setting the
scene. Candidate 6 is the shortest, but "more unreliable than my ex" leans
on a stock comparison. Candidate 8's "cool" pun is a pun everyone has
heard, which the judge brief ranks below freshness.
